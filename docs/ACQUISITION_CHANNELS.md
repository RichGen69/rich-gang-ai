are taken from the platform's own pages where possible; third-party
sources are labelled as such; anything not confirmed is marked
**UNVERIFIED**. Acquire.com is already done (separate track) and is

## ID TIMING — the decisive factor (added 2026-10-22)

Richard's government ID is **expired**, which is what blocks
Acquire's Persona verification. So every channel below was
re-checked for one question: is a valid government ID demanded
**before the listing goes live**, or only at closing/payout?

**Findings, in one line each:**
- **Little Exits:** no ID to list — its own Terms state "We do not
  vet, verify, validate, or conduct background checks on users."
- **Flippa:** no ID to list — Flippa's own support doc: "All users
  need to complete identity verification **before making or
  accepting a payment** on Flippa." ID gates the money, not the
  listing. But when it comes due, Flippa requires an **unexpired**
  document (its support pages say the ID "cannot be expired" /
  must be valid 6+ months for payments via Sumsub) — the expired ID
  fails there too, just later.
- **PreRevMarket:** no ID step appears anywhere in its public
  submission flow ("direct contact, no fees"). None found;
  UNVERIFIED beyond its public pages.
- **SideProjectors:** account signup only; no ID requirement found
  in its FAQ or reviews. UNVERIFIED beyond public pages.
- **BizBuySell:** no ID to list (the platform does not verify
  listings at all); only a payment card for the subscription.
- **ExitBid:** seller ID requirements UNVERIFIED — its materials
  tout "verified buyers" without stating a seller ID gate.
- **Direct pitch (Tiny form, strategic outreach, community posts):**
  no ID to pitch. ID enters only if a deal reaches a contract/
  escrow stage.

**The universal back-end rule:** any closing run through
**Escrow.com** — the default on Flippa and the standard for direct
deals — requires KYC with "a valid government-issued photo ID"
(Escrow.com's own identity-verification page). An expired ID will
not pass. **Strategy that follows: list and pitch everywhere NOW
with no ID; renew the ID in parallel; the renewed ID is only
actually needed the day a real buyer funds escrow.** Nothing below
requires routing around verification — it is sequencing, not
avoidance.

**Re-ranked for the expired-ID reality** (detail sections follow in
the original order; this table governs):
1. **Little Exits** — pre-revenue accepted + zero verification.
2. **Flippa** — pre-revenue accepted; ID only at payment stage.
3. **PreRevMarket** — free; no ID found.
4. **SideProjectors** — free; no ID found.
5. **ExitBid** — vendor claims; ID gate unverified.
6. **BizBuySell** — no ID, wrong audience.

## RANKED — marketplaces that will actually take this asset

### 1. Flippa — ACCEPTS. Best fit.
- **Pre-revenue eligibility:** Flippa sells a dedicated category for
  this exact case — demostack's read of Flippa's own pricing:
  "Starter Sites — $49 + 10% success fee — List starter websites and
  **pre-revenue digital businesses**." Multiple 2026 reviews agree
  there is no revenue minimum for self-serve listings.
- **Fees:** Listing fee by price band; at a $50,000 ask the band is
  $10K–$99.9K self-serve: **$49–$699 listing fee** (package-dependent)
  plus a **flat 10% success fee** on sales up to $500K (2026
  restructure, per ExitBid's fee analysis, which says it re-verified
  against Flippa's pricing page; Flippa's own site confirms selling
  packages "from $29" and success fees "from 5%" — the 5% applies
  only at $50M+, and the exact $50k-band package price should be
  confirmed on Flippa's pricing page at signup).
  On a $50,000 sale: ~$5,000 success fee + listing fee.
  Optional: confidential listing add-on $199; escrow via
  Escrow.com/FlippaPay costs extra (buyer-side rates ~0.5–1.2%,
  third-party figures).
- **What Richard must do personally:** create the Flippa account
  and **pay the listing fee by card** (non-refundable, even if it
  doesn't sell). **ID timing: NOT needed to list.** Flippa's own
  support documentation gates identity verification at payment
  ("before making or accepting a payment"), and requires the
  document to be unexpired when it is checked — so the renewed ID
  must exist before FlippaPay/Escrow.com releases funds, not before
  the listing goes live.
- **Verdict:** Largest buyer pool for sub-$100K digital assets and
  the only major marketplace that explicitly courts pre-revenue
  listings. List here first. Caveat from reviewers: Flippa does not
  verify sub-$50K listings, so expect low-quality inquiries mixed
  with real ones; the fixed $50k ask (not "make an offer") is the
  right posture.

### 2. Little Exits (formerly Tiny Acquisitions) — ACCEPTS.
- **Pre-revenue eligibility (platform's own FAQ, littleexits.com):**
  "Do you accept pre-revenue businesses? **Yes.** Little Exits helps
  founders sell pre-revenue businesses." The marketplace is
  purpose-built for online businesses **under $100k**.
- **Fees (own FAQ):** seller submission fee **$15** for listings
  above $1k (one-time, on submission for review) + **9.5% success
  fee** on completed transactions. Escrow is built into its process.
  On a $50,000 sale: $15 + $4,750.
- **What Richard must do personally:** create the account, submit
  the listing for review, pay the $15 submission fee. **ID timing:
  none to list** — Little Exits' own Terms of Service state it does
  not vet, verify, or background-check users. Any ID check would
  come only from the escrow provider at closing (which provider,
  and its exact KYC, UNVERIFIED).
- **Verdict:** Exact size band, explicit pre-revenue welcome, lowest
  upfront cost of any curated option. Smaller buyer pool than
  Flippa. List here second, in parallel (no exclusivity).

### 3. PreRevMarket (prerevmarket.com) — ACCEPTS. Free.
- **Pre-revenue eligibility (own homepage, fetched 2026-10-22):**
  "Discover online projects **without revenue** available for sale —
  direct contact, **no fees**, and founder friendly." This is the
  venue Microns.io itself redirects pre-revenue sellers to.
- **Fees:** $0 stated — no listing fee, no commission; deals are
  direct-contact, so Richard supplies his own escrow/contract at
  closing (same discipline as the direct-sale playbook).
- **What Richard must do personally:** submit the project form.
  **ID timing: none found** — no verification step appears in its
  public submission flow (UNVERIFIED beyond its public pages). Any
  closing Richard arranges directly should use escrow, which will
  require the renewed ID at that point.
- **Verdict:** Purpose-built for exactly this asset class and free —
  but small: live listings observed 2026-10-22 mostly ask
  $2,500–$27,900, so a $50k ask would sit at the very top of the
  board. Worth the ten minutes; expect discovery value, not depth
  of buyers.

### 4. SideProjectors — ACCEPTS. Free, unvetted.
- **Pre-revenue eligibility:** a developer listings board; its own
  FAQ prices projects "based on development hours and market value
  for newer projects" — no revenue requirement. Basic submissions
  are free (own FAQ: "Basic project submissions and browsing are
  free"; premium visibility features exist — current premium
  pricing **UNVERIFIED**, its pricing page was not captured).
- **Fees:** no listing fee and no commission on basic listings
  (consistent across the platform's FAQ and third-party reviews).
  No escrow, no vetting, no payment handling — closing is entirely
  private, so use an external escrow for any deal.
- **What Richard must do personally:** create the account and post.
  **ID timing: none found** — no ID requirement appears in its FAQ
  or coverage (UNVERIFIED beyond public pages). Closing is private,
  so the renewed ID matters only when his chosen escrow asks for
  it.
- **Verdict:** Zero cost, zero protection, low traffic. A
  classified ad, not a marketplace — post it and forget it.

### 5. ExitBid — CLAIMS to accept. Vendor claims UNVERIFIED.
- **Claims (all from ExitBid's own site, a competitor-comparison

- **What Richard must do personally:** account + the $199 fee, if

- **Verdict:** The auction format is the catch: price is discovered

### 6. BizBuySell — ACCEPTS technically. Poor fit.
- **Eligibility:** a general business-for-sale board; listings are
  **not verified** by the platform (its own review coverage states

  Technology category.
- **Fees (2026 reviews, consistent across two sources):** listing
  subscription **$74.95/mo Basic, $99.95/mo Showcase, $199.95/mo
  Diamond**, on 3/6/12-month terms; **no success commission**.
  Six months at Basic ≈ $450 burned whether or not a buyer appears.
- **What Richard must do personally:** account + monthly card
  subscription. **ID timing: none to list** — the platform does not
  verify listings; a payment card is the only credential involved.
- **Verdict:** Audience is brick-and-mortar and cash-flow buyers
  screening on SDE multiples; a $0-cash-flow code asset is
  invisible there. Last resort only.

## REJECTS — do not spend time or money

### Microns.io — REJECTS this asset (its own rules)
Microns' own submission page: "We don't post pre-revenue startups,
but we created PreRevMarket where you can submit projects without
revenue." Acceptance criteria require **bootstrapped
revenue-generating** businesses, a revenue/profit/traffic track
record, and **at least six months old**. THE ROOM fails all three
today ($0 revenue, founded this month). For the record, its seller
terms are good when eligible: free to list, 6–10% success fee.
**Revisit only after first real revenue exists.**

### Empire Flippers — REJECTS (official requirements)
Empire Flippers' own requirements page: a business must earn **at
least $2,000/month net profit averaged over 12 months**, have **12
months of revenue history**, and 3+ months of analytics. $0 revenue
= ineligible, full stop. (Its commission, ~15% blended on smaller
deals per third-party tables, is therefore moot.)

### FE International — REJECTS (own stated minimums)
FE International's own site: "as a minimum, your website would need
to generate **at least $500 per month in profit** before we can
consider selling your website," and its founder states SaaS work
generally starts **above $1,000 MRR**. A $0-revenue asset is below
both floors. Its commission is also not reliably published —
third-party figures conflict (5/4/3% tiered vs 10–15%) — marked
**UNVERIFIED** and moot at this revenue level.

### WebsiteClosers — REJECTS at this size/revenue (broker)
A broker, not a self-serve board: its live listings are
cash-flowing businesses (observed asks $900K–$10.5M with stated
cash flow), its SaaS sale checklist presumes 24+ months of
financials and MRR/churn metrics, and its commission is **not
published** ("negotiated by deal size," third-party). A pre-revenue
$50k asset is outside its engagement model.

### Honourable mention — IndieExit (indieexit.com)
Microns-family rules: "only a small percentage of exceptional
pre-revenue startups are accepted," requires 3+ months old (THE
ROOM is weeks old) and bans "adult entertainment" outright — the
21+ roster is a policy risk. Skip unless the other channels stall
and Richard wants to test it with the neutral copy.
