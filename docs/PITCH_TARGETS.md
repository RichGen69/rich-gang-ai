## Ranked targets — best fit first

### 1. Flippa — marketplace (Category 1)
- **What it is:** The largest open marketplace for buying/selling online businesses, SaaS, apps, and digital assets. Its browse categories include "AI Apps & Tools," "iOS & Android Apps," and — important for a pre-revenue asset — "Projects & Concepts." Flippa's own FAQ states sub-$50k deals are its fastest segment (median close 15 days for sub-$50k, ~49 days for $50k–$250k).
- **Fees (official pricing page):** Entry Level listing package starts at **$29**; "success fees start from 5%" (the pricing page shows a 10% success fee at lower asking-price tiers). Optional broker-led sale: $499 upfront + success fee. Payments via FlippaPay (from 1%) or Escrow.com (from 1.2%).
- **Entry:** https://flippa.com/pricing (use "Sell Now")
- **Why it fits:** Biggest buyer pool that explicitly trades in apps and pre-revenue "projects & concepts" at exactly this price point — no revenue requirement to list.
- Sources: https://flippa.com/pricing

### 2. Microns.io — micro-startup marketplace (Categories 1 + 3)
- **What it is:** A managed M&A marketplace purpose-built for micro-startups and side projects. Microns states its platform supports listings priced from **$1,000 to $500,000**, its buyers include indie makers, micro-PE funds, and serial acquirers, and its typical deal cycle is ~30 days. AI micro-startups are a called-out category.
- **Fees (Microns' own site):** Listing is **100% free**; Microns' sell guide says sellers are charged only once a project sells. Note: Microns' own blog posts conflict on whether a success commission applies (one post says no commission at all) — **confirm the current commission at listing time.**
- **Entry:** https://www.microns.io — seller guide: https://www.microns.io/blog/how-to-sell-your-side-project — about: https://www.microns.io/about
- **Why it fits:** Built for exactly this deal size and for founder-built projects; its buyer pool *is* the micro-acquirer crowd (see the Category 3 note below). Caveat: Microns markets "profitable" startups heavily, so the listing must lead with the IP/code value, not traction.
- Sources: https://www.microns.io/blog/microns-vs-microacquire-best-online-startup-marketplace ; https://www.microns.io/blog/how-to-sell-your-side-project

### 3. SideProjectors — side-project marketplace (Category 1)
- **What it is:** A marketplace and community specifically for buying and selling side projects, with categories for Mobile Apps (incl. Flutter) and AI & Machine Learning projects. All projects are reviewed by their team before listing; sellers submit with stack, metrics, and pricing.
- **Fees:** **No public fee schedule found** on the official site — check terms at signup.
- **Entry:** https://www.sideprojectors.com
- **Why it fits:** The one venue whose entire premise is selling projects like this one — a finished codebase from a solo builder — rather than operating businesses with P&Ls.
- Source: https://www.sideprojectors.com (FAQ, as indexed)

### 4. Deepgram — strategic acquirer, voice AI (Category 2)
- **Evidence of acquisition activity:** Deepgram's own company history page records "2024 — Deepgram acquires Poised"; its press release states the deal included Poised's "proprietary technology and integrations, as well as key members of its engineering team" — i.e., an assets/IP/talent acquisition, the same shape as this sale. Deepgram has since also acquired OfOne, a YC-backed voice-AI startup (announced alongside its $130M Series C).
- **Fit:** THE ROOM already integrates Deepgram (Nova-3 STT + Aura TTS) in its stack, so the codebase speaks their language; the character/voice-agent IP is adjacent to their voice-agent push.
- **Route:** **No public M&A/partnership submission route found.** Realistic path is direct outreach via the contact routes on https://deepgram.com
- **Honest odds:** Low — they buy voice products and teams, and this is a consumer character app — but the tech overlap makes it the most credible strategic name on this list.
- Sources: https://deepgram.com/company/history ; https://aithority.com/news/pioneering-voice-ai-provider-acquires-assets-ip-and-talent-to-enhance-user-interactions-in-virtual-meetings/

### 5. ElevenLabs — strategic acquirer, voice AI (Category 2)
- **Evidence of acquisition activity:** In Oct/Nov 2024 ElevenLabs acquired/hired the team behind Omnivore, the open-source read-it-later app, folding them into its ElevenReader product (TechCrunch). ElevenLabs also publicly staffs a Head of Partnerships (named in its PR Newswire releases) and announces enterprise partnerships regularly.
- **Fit:** THE ROOM's voice layer is ElevenLabs-based; its character-voice casting is built on their platform.
- **Route:** **No public M&A submission route found.** Outreach would be direct (website/LinkedIn); the only published ElevenLabs contact emails are press/partnership-function addresses in its press releases, not an acquisitions inbox.
- **Honest odds:** Low — the Omnivore deal was a team acqui-hire of a product already built on ElevenLabs' API. Included because it is a real, verified acquirer in this exact stack, not because a route is open.
- Sources: https://techcrunch.com/?p=2907694 ; https://www.advfn.com/stock-market/stock-news/97206724/elevenlabs-announces-enterprise-partnerships-with

### 6. SellMyApp — app source-code marketplace (Category 1)
- **What it is:** A long-running marketplace exclusively for buying/selling complete mobile app and game source code (iOS, Android, Unity). Per its company profile, it offers "full and exclusive rights" licensing options and **free registration, membership, and listings**.
- **Fees:** Sellers earn a tiered commission share of each sale; third-party figures conflict (60–80%) and were **not verified on the official site** — confirm current seller terms at signup.
- **Entry:** https://sellmyapp.com
- **Why it fits (with a caveat):** It is the most code-specific marketplace here, and exclusive-rights sales exist. But its engine is repeated template/license sales to many buyers, not a one-time $50k exclusive buyout — treat it as a fallback channel, structured as an exclusive-rights listing.
- Sources: https://sellmyapp.com ; https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/sell-my-app

### 7. Houston Angel Network (HAN) — investor route, Houston (Category 4)
- **What it is:** Houston's angel network; members invest individually in early-stage companies (equity/debt). **This is a funding route, not an asset buyer** — relevant only if the strategy shifts from selling the IP to raising money to commercialize it.
- **Terms (official entrepreneurs page):** **$100 application fee**; company must be early-stage with a **completed, working prototype and market validation (pilot, beta users, or revenue)** — exceptions may be granted. THE ROOM has the prototype but no market validation yet; that gap is the honest weak point of an application today.
- **Process:** Apply at least one month before the target pitch meeting; deal committees meet the last week of each month; pitch meetings are the third Wednesday monthly (except July and December); 3 companies pitch per meeting.
- **Entry:** https://www.houstonangelnetwork.org/entrepreneurs (application runs through Dealum after fee payment)
- Source: https://www.houstonangelnetwork.org/entrepreneurs

### 8. Central Texas Angel Network (CTAN) — investor route, Austin (Category 4)
- **What it is:** Austin-based angel group, ~140 accredited members, $142.4M invested in 233 companies per its own site; one of the most active angel networks in the US. Also an equity-funding route, not an asset purchase.
- **Terms (official site):** Runs **up to five funding cycles per year** (each at least six weeks); applications are submitted through its website (Dealum). A one-time administrative fee is charged **at closing**, tiered by capital invested by members: $2,000 on $50,000+, $5,000 on $100,000+, $10,500 on $250,000+, scaling up from there.
- **Entry:** https://www.ctan.com/funding-cycle-information — FAQ: https://www.ctan.com/faqs
- Sources: https://www.ctan.com/funding-cycle-information ; https://www.ctan.com/faqs

### 9. Y Combinator — accelerator (Category 4)
- **What it is:** The benchmark startup accelerator; funds companies (equity), does not buy assets. Included as the highest-value investor route for a solo AI founder willing to build the company instead of selling the asset.
- **Status (official apply page, checked 2026-10-22):** Accepting applications for the **Winter 2027 batch** (Jan–Mar 2027, San Francisco). **On-time deadline: November 2, 2026, 8pm PT** — decisions by December 11 for on-time applicants; late applications are still considered without a promised response date. Applications from future batches (Spring/Summer/Fall) are also open via Early Decision.
- **Entry:** https://www.ycombinator.com/apply
- **Action note:** This is the only time-critical item on the list — ~11 days from the research date to the on-time deadline.
- Source: https://www.ycombinator.com/apply

### 10. Capital Factory — Texas founder/investor network (Category 4)
- **What it is:** Texas-based (Austin HQ, statewide presence) venture fund + founder network. Its current front door is **All Access**: per Capital Factory's own page, "This isn't an accelerator and it isn't the average VC fund. The members are the network: hundreds of founders, operators, and investors… It's the network behind the Texas Fund."
- **Route:** https://capitalfactory.com/all-access — membership/network entry point to Texas investors; **no fixed application deadline is stated** (rolling network admission).
- **Why it's here:** The most direct standing connection between a Texas solo founder and Texas early-stage capital; a relationship route rather than a transaction route.
- Source: https://capitalfactory.com/all-access

### 11. DivInc — accelerator, Houston + Austin (Category 4)
- **What it is:** Texas accelerator (Houston programs based at the Ion) running themed 12-week cohorts (recent themes: Clean Energy, Women in Tech, Sports Tech, DWeb for Social Impact).
- **Eligibility (official FAQ — read carefully):** US-based, for-profit, scalable tech solution already built or an MVP; CEO must participate (25–30 hrs/week); **and "the participating founder must be a person of color or a woman entrepreneur."** If that criterion is met, this is a genuine Houston route; if not, it is closed by design.
- **Status:** **No current open-cohort deadline found** on the official site — cohorts are announced per theme at https://divinc.org
- **Entry:** https://divinc.org/faq
- Sources: https://divinc.org/faq ; https://divinc.org/blog/5-things-to-know-before-applying-to-divincs-dweb-for-social-impact-tech-accelerator

## Category 3 note — why there is no list of named micro-acquisition firms

The well-known software holding companies/acquirers were checked and **all require existing revenue or profit**, which a $0-revenue asset cannot meet:

- **Tiny** (tiny.com) — states it buys profitable SaaS with **$10M–$100M ARR**. Source: https://tiny.com/sell-your-saas
- **Kayra LLC** (kayrallc.com) — states it acquires products with **$1,000–$15,000 MRR** from real customers. Source: https://kayrallc.com
- **saas.group** — acquires profitable SaaS, typically **$2M–$10M ARR** per its founder's published description. Source: https://www.StarterStory.com/stories/saas-group

**Conclusion:** for a pre-revenue IP+code asset, the realistic micro-acquirer channel is not a named holding company — it is the indie-acquirer/micro-PE buyer pools on **Flippa (#1)** and **Microns (#2)** above.

## Checked and ruled out

- **Empire Flippers** — official listing requirements: at least **$2,000/month net profit averaged over 12 months** plus **12 months of revenue history**. $0 revenue does not qualify. Source: https://empireflippers.com/business-listing-requirements/
- **Motion Invest** — content websites only; an app/IP asset is out of scope. Source: https://www.microns.io/blog/microns-vs-empire-flippers-best-online-startup-marketplace-alternatives
- **FE International** (broker) — its valuation framework is built on SDE/earnings multiples of profitable businesses; a $0-profit asset has no basis for their process. Source: https://www.feinternational.com/blog/how-much-business-worth-valuation-2025
- **HeyGen** (avatar AI) — its only public "partner" route is an affiliate/referral program (35% commission on referred subscriptions); no acquisition activity or M&A route found. Source: https://www.heygen.com/en-gb/affiliate-program
- **Inworld AI** (character/voice AI) — does strategic technology partnerships (e.g., Tencent Cloud, Microsoft), but no public acquisition or partnership-submission route for selling IP was found. Sources: https://aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20260616ae82447/
- **Golden Section / Mercury Fund** (Houston VCs) — Golden Section is B2B-SaaS-only and Mercury writes ~$4M average checks into operating software companies; neither matches a $50k consumer-AI asset sale, and no public pitch-submission route was verified for either. Sources: https://www.rho.co/blog/vc-firms-in-houston-tx ; https://www.failory.com/blog/venture-capital-firms-houston
