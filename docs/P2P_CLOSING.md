## Closing sequence (no step skipped, no step reordered)

1. **Signed APA first.** A short Asset Purchase Agreement — asset
   definition (per the listing draft's deal structure), $50,000
   price, the chosen P2P rail, the payment schedule, and what is
   delivered at each stage — signed by both parties BEFORE any
   payment detail is exchanged.
2. **Handles stay inside the signed documents.** The seller's P2P
   handle is written into the APA only. It is never posted on the
   storefront, the listings, social media, or in cold outreach.
3. **Staged tranches matched to the rail.** Default structure:
   a deposit tranche, source-package delivery, then the balance
   tranche(s) on the schedule the rail's limits allow (single
   transaction only on PayPal, if it clears in one shot).
4. **Settled funds, not screenshots.** A stage counts as paid only
   when the money shows as received in the seller's own app AND a
   withdrawal to the seller's bank has been initiated. Screenshots,
   emails, and "pending" notices are not payment.
5. **Deliver per stage, in writing.** Each delivery (zip, credentials
   transfer, IP assignment) is acknowledged by the buyer in writing
   before the next tranche is requested.
6. **Close-out.** Final tranche settled → full delivery + a signed
   bill of sale / IP assignment → both parties keep the full paper
   trail (APA, payment confirmations, delivery receipts) for taxes.
   The income is taxable regardless of rail; large P2P receipts can
   trigger platform tax reporting (1099-K).

## Hard no's (these end the conversation with that buyer)

- Overpayment followed by "refund the difference" — the classic
  fake-payment pattern.
- A third party paying "on the buyer's behalf," or a payer name
  that doesn't match the APA signer.
- Any request to move an Acquire-sourced buyer off Acquire.
- Releasing the full source package before the agreed tranche has
  settled.
- Pressure to skip the APA "because P2P is casual." It isn't, at
  $50,000.
